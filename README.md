## Overview

This repository reviews published evidence on how virtual reality (VR) and augmented reality (AR) interact with human cognition, perception, neural activity, rehabilitation, presence, discomfort, and brain-health research.

The central question is: **what does published neuroscience and clinical research currently support about the short-term and longer-term effects of immersive VR/AR on the human brain, and where does the evidence remain uncertain?**

The work is relevant to **VR researchers, XR developers, Meta Quest engineers, HCI researchers, AI and tech builders, immersive-learning developers, neuroscientists, neurorehabilitation researchers, technical educators, and VR enthusiasts**.

## Evidence standard

This repository is an **independent narrative evidence review**. It is not presented as a journal, and it does not claim a PRISMA-compliant systematic review unless a complete protocol, database search strategy, screening flow, inclusion/exclusion criteria, extracted dataset, and reproducible study count are published.

Earlier versions used counts such as “285+ studies synthesized.” That wording was not adequately supported by the repository's published methodology and has been removed.

Where quantitative findings are discussed, they should be traceable to the cited source. For example, a 2025 meta-analysis of VR interventions for mild cognitive impairment included **30 randomized controlled trials and 1,365 participants**; that figure belongs to that individual meta-analysis, not to this repository's own study count.

## Current evidence frame

Published reviews support a cautious picture:

- VR is widely studied in neuroscience, rehabilitation and psychological intervention research.
- Some interventions show benefits for selected cognitive, rehabilitation, pain or anxiety outcomes.
- Effects depend strongly on population, intervention design, level of immersion, duration, hardware and outcome measure.
- Cybersickness and other adverse effects remain relevant human-factors issues.
- Evidence about long-term brain-health effects is substantially less complete than evidence about short-term performance, symptoms or rehabilitation outcomes.
- Neural changes reported in particular studies should not be generalized to all VR use.

## What this repo is not

- It is **not** a medical guideline.
- It is **not** a clinical recommendation.
- It is **not** a peer-reviewed journal article.
- It is **not** evidence that VR universally improves or harms the brain.
- It is **not** a systematic review unless a reproducible review protocol is published.
